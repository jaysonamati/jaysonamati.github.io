- To understand the limits of a formal system (e.g mathematics) one needs to understand it's foundations (e.g set theory or HoTT or [HOTT](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/higher+observational+type+theory)).
    - Whether one **only** needs to understand the foundations is not resolved for me.
    - This could be one way in which to "break" out of the formal system.
    - Does this mean then that to develop a "new mathematics" it is sufficient to develop novel foundations?
        - Foundations both for "computers" and also for our minds.
    - Wonder what [Godel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_G%C3%B6del) has to say about this.
- Software as a living computational medium.
    - Malleable computing...
- There is this reduction of time from thought to actualization, particularly in computer systems that makes the computers feel malleable.
  From a programming perspective this feels great compared to 3 yrs ago (even 2, even 1) when the lag between "seeing" a program in the mind
  and excecuting that program was large. Now it is almost immediate, there is virtually no lag for some programs, there are countless of anecdotes
  of people one-shoting apps.
    - I like to view this as the computation age finally coming to its own. Computation is finally a medium.
        - Similar to how literacy levels made reading and writing a medium of being, computation is becoming a medium for being.
        - A few years ago this was reserved to "high priest programmers".
    - A lag that is finally visible is that of fit, impact and actualizing the potential of an idea or thought.
        - One has (had) to create an organization around an application (or invention) in order for it to "scale".
            - This does not need to be the case.
        - Creating and running an organization (excecution) is laggy.
            - How do we eliminate this "real world" lag?
            - Or at least reduce it significantly.
            - a potential:
                - Creating a computational substrate through which organizations can form, coordinate, adapt, and dissolve with far less administrative cost.
                - Imagine an org existing for 30 seconds, but creating meaningful value in that time... the same can be said for programs.
                    - Or existing for a million years or so...
                - Basically organizing at the speed of information.
                - [e](https://page.twentythreeinreverse.com/) has some aspects of this.
                    - Also happens to be a cool decentralized program (has a bit of everything).
- The [information ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_ecology) is a _______ minefield. Craft your mind to be a tank, slogging through the muck.
    - Adapting while moving.
    - Real-time evolution.
